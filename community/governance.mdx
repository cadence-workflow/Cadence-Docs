This document outlines the governance model for the Cadence Workflow project. It defines the roles, responsibilities, decision-making processes, and contribution guidelines to ensure transparency, inclusivity, and sustainability within the Cadence open-source community.

---

## Guiding Principles

The governance of the Cadence project adheres to the following principles:

1. **Meritocracy**: Decisions are guided by technical merit and the value of contributions to the community.  
2. **Transparency**: Discussions, decisions, and contributions are made in public forums.  
3. **Inclusivity**: Contributions are welcome from anyone, regardless of affiliation, experience, or background.  
4. **Sustainability**: Processes are designed to ensure the long-term health and success of the project.

### Meritocracy
Any official role (defined below) in the Cadence project requires a proven track record of contributions. 

Cadence is used in many tier 0-2 services across many companies, which are part of their infra/foundation or core business. This is also necessary to keep Cadence reliable and trustable. We would like to grow our community with people who care about the project and put careful effort into improving it.

Primary contributions are commits, publications in official Cadence mediums (GitHub, cadenceworkflow.io, etc.), or community support (over Slack, GitHub, Stack Overflow, etc.) where the Cadence community can directly benefit. 

To become eligible for an official role, contributions should sustain for at least 6 months with a convincing case for future commitment. For example, to become a maintainer, a contributor should have regular commits within the last 6 months and should explain how they will continue contributing. 

Candidates need to join at least one community meeting to gain familiarity from other members in related roles and to contribute in project processes.

After being eligible, a contributor could apply to have an official role. Anyone with an official role should disclose their real name publicly apart from their GitHub aliases and provide their emails to the Cadence team for contact purposes. The Technical Steering Committee will then hold a voting process to move forward or to give feedback. 

### Transparency
Plans, roadmaps, and projects are posted publicly. Similarly, Cadence community meetings are open to everyone. Planning discussions are held in community meetings. People with official roles are published publicly. Lastly, our decision-making process is documented transparently (explained below). 

Our decisions and processes are explained with clear reasons, motivation, and goals, which are all based on addressing community needs and improving our technology.

### Inclusivity
The Cadence project strives to have contributions from more and diverse contributors. We believe we can build our best only if we can receive inputs from more people.

Basic contributions such as sending PRs, leaving reviews, creating or responding to issues, and submitting project proposals are open to everyone without requiring any prior approval.

Official roles are open to everyone regardless of affiliation, experience, or background. Our criteria will be merely based on the objective requirements mentioned above.

### Sustainability
Processes are designed to ensure the long-term health and success of the project.

We aim to avoid bugs, security issues, unfunded projects, half-done projects, unmaintained issues, and launches without ownership. A clear understanding of maintainability is needed for any project's approval.

We strive to simplify our project to make it easier to understand and contribute to, and to avoid having single points of failure.

## Official Roles

The current TSC members and maintainers are listed in [MAINTAINERS.md](https://github.com/cadence-workflow/cadence/blob/master/MAINTAINERS.md). Profiles on the [team page](/community/team) can be out of date.

### Maintainer
A maintainer is a person who understands the Cadence codebase, with consistent past contributions. Maintainer rights and responsibilities are as follows:
  * Merge rights (code-review is still required in most cases),
  * Review Contributions,
  * Fix Bugs and Update Dependencies,
  * Release Updates,
  * Manage Issues,
  * Enforce Standards

Eligibility requirements are:
  * Minimum 6 months of consistent code contributions (PRs)
  * Attendance in community meetings to talk about updates and provide their inputs
  * Voted approval from Technical Steering Committee

### Member of the Technical Steering Committee (TSC)
Cadence Technical Steering Committee
  * Sets technical vision and strategy, 
  * Makes technical decisions, 
  * Approves design documents, 
  * Resolves technical disagreements, 
  * Contributes to planning, 
  * Govern standards and practices,
  * Facilitates contributions

A person is eligible for the TSC in either of these ways:

  * They are already a maintainer, with 2+ approved large project proposals and involvement in 2 large projects’ designs.
  * They have consistent, long-term involvement in the community and in steering the project.

Either path also requires attendance in community meetings to talk about updates and provide their inputs, and voted approval from the Technical Steering Committee.

### Leaving an official role
An official role belongs to the person.

A maintainer or TSC member leaves the role in any of these cases:

  * They step down in writing or in public. The TSC records the step-down.
  * A maintainer leaves the Cadence team at a company that funds their Cadence work. The TSC may end the maintainer role at once. The person has two weeks to notify the TSC that they will keep contributing. That notice reverses the removal.
  * A maintainer is inactive for six months. Inactivity means no pull requests, reviews, or issue work. This inactivity rule does not apply to TSC members.
  * The TSC votes to end the role. The TSC can vote any maintainer out when it considers that necessary for the project, including for community health, collaboration, quality, or another reason recorded with the vote. The TSC can likewise vote a member out of the committee.

Leaving a funded Cadence team can end the maintainer role as soon as the TSC records it. That two-week window is for the person to stay a maintainer, not a waiting period before the TSC can act. If they notify within two weeks, the removal is reversed and maintainership is reinstated. They open a GitHub issue in [cadence](https://github.com/cadence-workflow/cadence) or [TSC-Discussions](https://github.com/cadence-workflow/TSC-Discussions). No confirmation is required for that notification.

A TSC member who leaves a funded Cadence team keeps the TSC seat until the TSC votes through an issue or a pull request.

Six months of inactivity lets the TSC end a maintainer role. The change is a [MAINTAINERS.md](https://github.com/cadence-workflow/cadence/blob/master/MAINTAINERS.md) update with two TSC members involved: a TSC member opens the pull request and another TSC member approves it, or someone else opens the pull request and two or more TSC members approve it.

A vote to end a role uses the recorded vote, or an issue or pull request for a TSC member who left a funded team. The TSC states the reason in the TSC repository. Voting a person out of the TSC leaves their maintainer rights in place unless that vote also ends those rights.

After the role ends, the TSC may list the person as emeritus on the [team page](/community/team). Emeritus recognizes past work. It does not include merge rights or a vote. Returning to an active role follows the eligibility rules for that role and a new TSC vote, except for a maintainer reinstated by a notice within two weeks. Any maintainer who lost the role within the last six months can ask to be voted in again.

## Contribution Guidelines

1. **How to Contribute**:  
   * Review the [contributing guide](https://github.com/cadence-workflow/cadence/blob/master/CONTRIBUTING.md).  
   * Submit issues to report bugs, suggest features, or ask questions.  
   * Create pull requests for code, documentation, or tests.  
2. **Review Process**:  
   * All pull requests must be reviewed by at least one maintainer.
   * Significant changes require approval from the TSC.
   * Based on your past contributions and future commitments, you may be invited to be a part of Cadence open source teams listed in [cadence-workflow](https://github.com/cadence-workflow) org.
3. **Code Standards**:  
   * Follow the project's coding styles and guidelines.  
   * Ensure code includes adequate tests and documentation.  
4. **Behavior Expectations**:  
   * Abide by the [Code of Conduct](https://github.com/uber/.github/blob/dcd96c52f2d1d839208315a2572cf37f48e52e96/CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).  
   * Treat all community members with respect and professionalism.

## Governing Model & Voting Process
Cadence follows an **electoral** governing model where official voting will be held for certain decisions such as becoming an official member, technical discussions (major changes, controversial features, features deprecations, major releases etc.), planning, policy etc. 

A member of TSC can call for voting for a subject in case they want an official opinion from every member. Before any election, two weeks' notice should be given to members.

Voting can be held online or offline if a member cannot make the meeting or if a common meeting time cannot be found. Even if the voting happens offline, members can still partially meet online to discuss the case. 

Each election should be posted in a TSC dedicated GitHub repo. Each member is reached via their email, which is shared with every TSC member. It’s the member’s responsibility to provide a working email address in case the email bounces back. The member who calls for voting sends email notifications. 

Voting has to be recorded and should clearly show a vote was cast from the voter's account (email or GitHub or another account officially linked to the voter). A vote could be "in favor", "not in favor", or "abstain". When the vote is a pull request, a TSC member's approval counts as "in favor". Requesting changes counts as "not in favor".

Each company counts at most two TSC votes. The majority is calculated from that company-capped total, not from headcount. Count each company's ballots as follows:

  * If there is at least one vote in favor and at least one not in favor, count one in favor and one not in favor. Ignore abstains from that company.
  * If there are votes on only one side, in favor or not in favor, plus abstains, count one vote on that side and one abstain.
  * If there are only abstains, only in favor or only not in favor, count up to two of those votes.

Abstains do not choose a side. They count toward whether the TSC has received enough votes to decide. The outcome is still the majority of counted "in favor" and "not in favor" votes.

In case a response wasn't received from all the members within two weeks, all the pending votes, with the 2-week notice period already expired, will be cast by the end of the first day (Pacific Time) of the coming month. This way, every member can expect a voting might be happening and they can find voting issues in the dedicated repo. Decisions will be made based on received votes at that time. 

At any time after the initial notification, if more than half of the company-capped votes have been received, including counted abstains, a decision can be made without waiting for other members, since their vote will have no effect.

Otherwise, voting results are decided based on the counted "favor" and "not in favor" votes after the company cap. Whichever vote has a majority will be the decision. If no majority is reached, TSC can continue with a re-election or status quo is maintained (i.e., no change).

Every decision should be published at a TSC GitHub repo with vote numbers for TSC visibility, and announced publicly.

## **Project Roadmap**

The TSC owns the roadmap. The roadmap outlines:

* Upcoming features and milestones.  
* Technical debt and areas of improvement.  
* Long-term goals for the project.

The TSC defines and refreshes the roadmap once a year. It may soft-refresh priorities around the middle of the year. A soft refresh adjusts emphasis inside the year's scope. The TSC can also accept a project at any time during the year when its importance and funding support it. Changing the year's scope uses the recorded vote above. The vote-free path is for accepting a project only.

Accepting a project uses the recorded vote, or a vote-free path that assumes good faith so work is not blocked. A TSC member who proposes a project and volunteers for it accepts it without another TSC member's approval. A maintainer or a company that volunteers needs at least one TSC member to accept that offer in writing or in public. There is no pushback window before acceptance. After the fact, a TSC member can push back and open the acceptance to discussion and a recorded vote. That vote can reverse the acceptance, including progress already made.

A TSC member opens the annual proposal in [cadence-workflow/TSC-Discussions](https://github.com/cadence-workflow/TSC-Discussions). The [2027 roadmap proposal](https://github.com/cadence-workflow/TSC-Discussions/issues/3) is the current example.

After the year's scope is approved, and whenever an idea comes up later, the project collects ideas in public. An idea is raised on GitHub, for example on the [2027 call for project ideas](https://github.com/cadence-workflow/cadence/issues/8533). A conversation in [CNCF Slack `#cadence-users`](https://inviter.co/cncf) or in a community meeting becomes an idea when a maintainer records it on GitHub.

Maintainers and other community members can comment, volunteer, and vote on an idea. That vote shows popularity and interest. The TSC uses it as input. The TSC accepts a project with its recorded vote, or on the vote-free path above. The TSC then names an owner. Volunteering before acceptance shows interest.

Day-to-day bug reports, small improvements, and release work continue through GitHub issues. The roadmap is the commitment for larger, multi-month work.

Status is tracked on the [Cadence GitHub projects](https://github.com/orgs/cadence-workflow/projects). The [Roadmap](https://github.com/orgs/cadence-workflow/projects/1) board is the year's plan. High-level updates are also posted on the [project website](/). The process is described in [Roadmap process](/docs/tech-review/day-0-planning/scope/roadmap-process).

# Cadence™ at KubeCon® North America 2026

It has been just over a week since KubeCon + CloudNativeCon® North America 2026 wrapped up in Salt Lake City, and it was an exciting, busy week for the Cadence project. Across [five sessions](https://events.linuxfoundation.org/kubecon-cloudnativecon-north-america/program/schedule/?search=cadence), we talked about **agentic workflows**, the **pitfalls** teams run into when taking them to production, and how Cadence helps. We also spent plenty of time listening to how other projects and companies are tackling similar problems. Along the way, we shared **new subprojects** that help engineers build workflows and strengthen their distributed services, introduced our **Adopter Program**, and previewed upcoming **community meetups**. This post reflects on our experience and the impressions we brought home from KubeCon NA 2026.

## Cadence Sessions

### Secure Your Agents in 5 Minutes: Durable Execution with Cadence

Diana Zawadzki [introduced](https://events.linuxfoundation.org/kubecon-cloudnativecon-north-america/program/schedule/?id=1289877) how **durable execution** addresses common failures in AI agent workflows. Model calls fail, tools time out, workers restart, and human approvals can take hours. The lightning talk covered how Cadence preserves **workflow state** and provides **retries**, **timeouts**, **recovery**, and **human checkpoints**, helping developers build agents that can continue making progress through interruptions.

### Agents Fail. Workflows Recover. Durable Agent Orchestration with Cadence

[Diana Zawadzki and Shijie Sheng](https://events.linuxfoundation.org/kubecon-cloudnativecon-north-america/program/schedule/?id=1238002) showed how familiar **workflow patterns** make agents more reliable. In an **on-call analyzer** demo, they retried rate-limited requests, guarded against repeated lookups, added **human review**, and stopped the worker. Cadence preserved completed model and tool results so the same run could **resume** after an interruption or wait for guidance without starting over.

The production example was Uber®’s **Agent Execution Service (AES)**, which uses Cadence for background and scheduled agent tasks, with contributions from Sean Po, Ethan Lee, Daniel Yao, and the Michelangelo team. Inspecting **execution histories** and agent inputs and outputs helped narrow a production timeout anomaly to one worker pool. A later **30-day snapshot** recorded no failed or timed-out analyzer runs; the reason for that change still needs investigation.

Agents fail. Cadence recovers workflows. Explore the [on-call example](https://github.com/cadence-workflow/cadence-ai-samples/tree/main/recipes/incident-triage).  

### Splitting the Monolith at Cloudera®: Building Fault-Tolerant Workflows with Cadence

Soma Koltai and Ender Demirkaya [shared](https://events.linuxfoundation.org/kubecon-cloudnativecon-north-america/program/schedule/?id=1247100) Cloudera’s migration from a **monolithic executor** to Cadence for long-running provisioning operations. The session covered the **Lease API** for distributed locking and an intermediary **Async Executor** that enabled an incremental transition. The case study described how a severe memory leak and subsequent node rescheduling resulted in **zero downtime** for user workloads, demonstrating the practical value of making infrastructure operations recoverable.

### Building and Maintaining Production-Ready SDKs with LLMs

Ender Demirkaya and Shijie Sheng presented at [Agentics Day: MCP™ \+ Agents](https://events.linuxfoundation.org/kubecon-cloudnativecon-north-america/co-located-events/cncf-hosted-co-located-schedule/?id=1250590&utm_source=chatgpt.com), sharing how the Cadence community is applying **LLMs** to SDK development and maintenance across programming languages. The session covered a pipeline that transforms an existing SDK into a **language-agnostic representation**, adds semantic metadata, and applies language-specific rules to generate SDKs for target languages. Most of the process runs **locally** and can produce a new SDK in **hours**. The talk explored the architecture, implementation challenges, and lessons learned while preserving **API consistency** and each language’s conventions.

### Cadence Workflow ContribFest: Build Durable AI Agent Examples

Diana Zawadzki and Shijie Sheng [led a hands-on session](https://events.linuxfoundation.org/kubecon-cloudnativecon-north-america/program/schedule/?id=1294751) focused on building and improving **durable AI workflow** examples. The introductory **Incident Triage** exercise brought together unreliable APIs, model calls, and human decisions, with participants inspecting execution in **Cadence Web** and adjusting a **retry policy**. The session offered opportunities to extend examples, bring new workflow ideas, and contribute with **maintainer guidance**. Explore the [ContribFest guide](https://github.com/cadence-workflow/cadence-ai-samples/blob/main/CONTRIBFEST.md?utm_source=chatgpt.com) to continue contributing.

## Announcements

### Shard Manager

Our recent [Shard Manager announcement](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7526829924813524992/) brings work developed for Cadence to a broader set of distributed systems. Shard Manager is a **standalone, general-purpose** service that teams can use without adopting Cadence. It balances shards by **observed load** and provides a central view of ownership and placement, along with controls for **draining** hosts or individual shards.

In Uber’s Cadence Matching rollout, **load-aware placement** reduced CPU utilization on the hottest host by **38%**. The announcement received **700+ likes** and **18+ reshares**. Thank you to everyone who shared it and reached out. Read the [design and production results](https://cadenceworkflow.io/blog/2026/10/30/balancing-cadence-shards-with-shard-manager/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) to learn more.

### Workflow Home Pages

[Workflow Home Pages](https://cadenceworkflow.io/docs/concepts/workflow-home-page/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) lets developers define a custom interface directly inside **Cadence Web** using **Markdown** and interactive controls. A workflow’s home page can display its current state, collect input, provide **approval and rejection** buttons, start new workflows, and list related executions.

For suitable use cases, this can mean **one less service** to build, deploy, and maintain. The workflow defines how work progresses, while its home page gives people a place to understand that progress and take action.

### Cadence Adopter Program

The [Cadence Adopter Program](https://cadenceworkflow.io/community/adopter-program/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) offers **free, direct maintainer assistance** to teams bringing Cadence into production. Maintainers help with **deployment**, **configuration**, **capacity planning**, and **provisioning** blockers, working alongside adopters until their clusters are successfully provisioned.

Assistance is offered **first come, first served**, subject to maintainer availability. Teams provide and operate their infrastructure, while maintainers contribute their time and experience. The collaboration also helps us improve **documentation**, **automation**, and the **deployment experience** for future adopters.

## What we learned

Across the conference program and public community recaps, a common theme emerged: **AI adoption** is making familiar engineering concerns more urgent. **Security**, **recovery**, **observability**, **cost**, and **developer experience** all matter when moving from an experiment to a system people depend on.

### Responsible AI requires controls throughout execution

As AI use matures, responsible adoption requires clear boundaries around what agents can do. **Isolation**, **governance**, **security**, and **accountability** need to work alongside **recovery**, **human control**, **observability**, and consistent **policy enforcement**. These capabilities belong throughout the **execution lifecycle**, from the initial request to the final action.

### Identity and permissions must follow the work

An agent’s **permissions** should reflect its task, the person it represents, and the systems it needs to reach. Those permissions may change while the agent is running. Long-running workflows need to respect updated **access rules**, preserve the **chain of responsibility**, and make it possible to understand who authorized each action.

### Efficiency should be measured against completed work

The cost of an AI application includes **model calls**, idle infrastructure, repeated operations, storage, and observability. Measuring **cost per reliably completed task** gives teams a more useful view of efficiency. It also encourages designs that **preserve progress**, avoid unnecessary calls, and release resources while waiting.

### Open standards need consistent behavior

Supporting the same protocol or data format is a starting point. Practical **interoperability** also depends on **consistent semantics**, clear compatibility expectations, and useful context across components. Teams benefit when they can change models, tools, or infrastructure without rebuilding their application’s **operational foundations**.

### Governance needs a good developer experience

Controls are easier to adopt when developers understand them. **Clear feedback**, **usable defaults**, **actionable error messages**, and straightforward **onboarding** help teams work within established boundaries. Good tooling makes responsible development easier to practice every day.

### Uber open sourced Michelangelo AI

Uber announced the **open sourcing** of [Michelangelo AI](https://michelangelo-ai.org/docs/?utm_source=chatgpt.com), its platform for building, deploying, and managing machine learning models. It brings together **data preparation**, **training**, **evaluation**, **deployment**, and **monitoring**, drawing on years of production experience at Uber. Making that work available gives other teams a foundation for building **ML infrastructure** and an opportunity to contribute their own experience.

### Kubernetes® is adapting to new kinds of workloads

AI is pushing Kubernetes beyond the assumptions of conventional services. **Agent Substrate™** addresses the execution and isolation needs of agents, while projects such as **KubeRay** and **OpenRL™** support distributed computing and reinforcement learning. These developments show the community extending Kubernetes to handle workloads with different **lifecycles**, **resource requirements**, and **failure patterns**.

### GPU efficiency is driving changes in scheduling

Running AI efficiently involves more than adding accelerators. Workloads need the right **hardware**, **networking**, **placement**, and **sharing policies**. **Dynamic Resource Allocation** allows applications to describe their hardware requirements more precisely, and managed implementations are bringing those capabilities into wider use. The interesting shift is toward scheduling based on **workload needs** and **hardware capabilities**, with **utilization** and **operational cost** shaping the design.

### Platform engineering is delivering measurable improvements

Meta® shared how it brought routine application deployments down from more than **four hours to minutes**. Previously, application updates ran through a **monolithic Terraform® pipeline**, where one stalled application could block unrelated releases. The team kept Terraform for infrastructure provisioning and moved application delivery to **Argo® CD** and **Kargo**, giving applications independent promotion processes and health checks. [CNCF®](https://www.cncf.io/announcements/2026/11/11/meta-wins-cncf-end-user-case-study-contest-for-accelerating-ai-infrastructure-deployments-from-hours-to-minutes/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

What made the approach interesting was how they integrated it with Meta’s existing systems. Developers provide a short **application definition**, and the platform generates the deployment configuration. Selected internal configuration and infrastructure data flow into Git through **one-way synchronization**. Kargo writes a complete desired-release record into Git, which Argo CD reconciles against the cluster, making releases easier to **trace and reconstruct**. The takeaway is how much value comes from removing dependencies between unrelated deployments while keeping the **developer interface** simple.

### Observability is evolving in two directions

Teams are building **observability for AI applications** while also applying **AI to investigate** distributed systems. Jaeger™ previewed experimental capabilities for **natural-language troubleshooting**, **MCP tools**, and visualization of **generative AI traces**. This creates useful possibilities for understanding complex executions, while keeping the underlying evidence accessible to engineers who need to verify an explanation.

### Safer operations are becoming part of the platform

**Fleet management**, **drift detection**, and **automated upgrade checks** showed continued investment in everyday operational reliability. Microsoft®’s announcements included controls for managing workloads across clusters and **health gates** that stop upgrades from progressing when conditions deteriorate. Our takeaway is that platforms become more useful when operational knowledge moves from manual procedures into **repeatable, observable controls**.
